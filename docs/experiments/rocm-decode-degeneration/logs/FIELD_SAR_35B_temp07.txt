Maxwell's equations are a set of four partial differential equations that form the foundation of **classical electromagnetism**. Published by Scottish physicist James Clerk Maxwell between 1861 and 1865, they unified electricity, magnetism, and optics into a single coherent theory and remain one of the most celebrated achievements in physics.

Below is a clear, physically intuitive overview (using SI units and modern differential form).

---

### 🔹 The Four Equations

| # | Name | Equation (Differential Form) | Physical Meaning |
|---|------|------------------------------|------------------|
| 1 | **Gauss's Law (Electricity)** | `∇·E = ρ/ε₀` | Electric charges produce electric fields. The total "flux" of **E** out of a closed region equals the enclosed charge divided by the vacuum permittivity `ε₀`. |
| 2 | **Gauss's Law (Magnetism)** | `∇·B = 0` | There are no magnetic monopoles. Magnetic field lines form continuous closed loops; they never start or end at a point. |
| 3 | **Faraday's Law of Induction** | `∇×E = −∂B/∂t` | A changing magnetic field induces a circulating (curling) electric field. This is the principle behind electric generators and transformers. |
| 4 | **Ampère-Maxwell Law** | `∇×B = μ₀J + μ₀ε₀ ∂E/∂t` | Magnetic fields are produced by electric currents (`J`) **and** by changing electric fields (the second term, called the *displacement current*, was Maxwell's crucial addition). |

**Notation key:**
- `E` = electric field (V/m)
- `B` = magnetic field (T)
- `ρ` = electric charge density (C/m³)
- `J` = current density (A/m²)
- `ε₀` = vacuum permittivity (~8.85×10⁻¹² F/m)
- `μ₀` = vacuum permeability (4π×10⁻⁷ H/m)
- `∇·` = divergence (measures "sources" or "sinks")
- `∇×` = curl (measures "rotation" or "circulation")
- `∂/∂t` = partial derivative with respect to time

---

### 🔑 Key Implications

1. **Electromagnetic Waves**: When you combine these equations (especially Faraday's and Ampère-Maxwell laws), they predict that oscillating `E` and `B` fields can propagate through empty space as self-sustaining waves.
2. **Light is an EM Wave**: Solving the wave equation yields a propagation speed `c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀)`, which matched the measured speed of light. Maxwell concluded that **light itself is an electromagnetic wave**, unifying optics with electricity and magnetism.
3. **Gauge Invariance & Field Theory**: Maxwell's equations were the first successful "field theory," replacing action-at-a-distance with local field interactions. This deeply influenced later theories (relativity, quantum field theory).
4. **Lorentz Invariance**: The equations naturally respect the speed-of-light limit and are invariant under Lorentz transformations. This made them a direct precursor to Einstein's special relativity (1905).

---

### 🌍 Modern Context & Limitations

- **Classical Realm**: Maxwell's equations work perfectly for macroscopic phenomena (antennas, optics, power grids, radar, etc.).
- **Quantum Realm**: At atomic scales, they are replaced (or refined) by **Quantum Electrodynamics (QED)**, which describes how photons and charged particles interact. Maxwell's equations emerge as the classical limit of QED.
- **Relativistic Form**: In modern physics, they are often written compactly using tensors and 4-vectors:  
  `∂_μ F^{μν} = μ₀ J^ν` and `∂_[α F_{βγ]} = 0` (covariant form), making Lorentz invariance explicit.
- **Known Limits**: They break down at singularities (e.g., point charges) and don't account for quantum effects like vacuum polarization or particle creation/annihilation.

---

### 💡 Why They Matter

- **Technology**: Every wireless system (Wi-Fi, cellular, GPS, radio), power grid, motor, generator, and optical fiber relies on them.
- **Physics Paradigm**: They demonstrated that nature's forces could be described by elegant, universal field equations, setting the template for all modern field theories (including the Standard Model).
- **Unification**: Maxwell was the first to show that seemingly distinct phenomena (electricity, magnetism, light) are manifestations of a single underlying reality.

---

- **Liénard–Wiechert potentials**: Describe fields of moving charges (retarded time).

Fourier analysis is a mathematical framework for **decomposing functions or signals into pure oscillatory components** (sines and cosines). It reveals the hidden "frequency content" of data and turns complex operations (like differentiation or convolution) into simple algebraic manipulations.

Below is a structured overview covering intuition, formalism, key properties, applications, and modern context.

---

### 🔍 Core Idea & Intuition
Imagine a musical chord: multiple notes played simultaneously. **Fourier analysis is a "frequency microscope"** that tells you exactly which frequencies are present and how much "weight" each carries. Mathematically, it expresses any reasonable function as a sum (or integral) of sinusoids, which are the natural "building blocks" of linear, time-invariant systems.

---

### 📐 The Three Mathematical Pillars

| Form | When to Use | Formula (Angular Frequency ω) |
|------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| **Fourier Series** | Periodic functions (`f(t+T)=f(t)`) | `f(t) = Σₙ cₙ e^{i n ω₀ t}`, `ω₀=2π/T`<br>`cₙ = (1/T) ∫₀ᵀ f(t) e^{-i n ω₀ t} dt` |
| **Fourier Transform (FT)** | Non-periodic, finite-energy signals | `F(ω) = ∫_{-∞}^{∞} f(t) e^{-iωt} dt`<br>`f(t) = (1/2π) ∫ F(ω) e^{iωt} dω` |
| **Discrete FT (DFT/FFT)** | Sampled digital data (`N` points) | `F_k = Σ_{n=0}^{N-1} f_n e^{-i 2πkn/N}`<br>Inverse: `f_n = (1/N) Σ F_k e^{i 2πkn/N}` |

**Note:** There are multiple conventions (scaling factors `1/2π`, `1/√2π`, or frequency `ν` vs `ω`). The angular frequency `ω` convention above is standard in physics/engineering.

---

### ⚙️ Key Properties & Theorems
These make Fourier analysis computationally and theoretically powerful:

| Property | Statement | Why It Matters |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| **Linearity** | `F{af+bg} = aF{f} + bF{g}` | Superposition holds; systems decompose cleanly. |
| **Convolution Theorem** | `F{f * g} = F{f} · F{g}` | Turns integral convolutions into pointwise multiplication. Core to filtering & linear systems. |
| **Shift/Modulation** | `F{f(t-t₀)} = e^{-iωt₀}F(ω)`<br>`F{e^{iω₀t}f(t)} = F(ω-ω₀)` | Time delay → phase shift; frequency shift → spectrum translation. |
| **Parseval/Plancherel** | `∫|f(t)|²dt = (1/2π)∫|F(ω)|²dω` | Energy is conserved between time & frequency domains. Basis for signal power analysis. |
| **Differentiation** | `F{f'(t)} = iω F(ω)` | Derivatives become multiplication by `iω`. Diagonalizes linear differential equations. |

---

### 🌍 Major Applications
- **Signal Processing:** Filtering, noise reduction, spectral analysis, communications (OFDM, modulation)
- **Physics & Engineering:** Solving PDEs (heat, wave, Schrödinger equations), optics (Fourier optics, diffraction), control theory
- **Data Compression:** JPEG (uses DCT, a Fourier variant), MP3 (psychoacoustic filtering in frequency domain)
- **Numerical Analysis:** Spectral methods for PDEs, fast solvers (FFT-based Poisson solvers)
- **Probability/Statistics:** Characteristic functions (`φ(t)=E[e^{itX}]`), central limit theorem proofs
- **Quantum Mechanics:** Position ↔ momentum duality (`ψ(x)` ↔ `φ(p)`) is a Fourier pair.

---

### ⚠️ Limitations & Modern Extensions
| Limitation | Modern Solution |
|------------|-----------------|
| **Global support:** Standard FT assumes stationarity (frequency doesn't change over time). | **Short-Time FT (STFT)**, **Wavelets** (localized time-frequency analysis) |
| **Boundary/periodicity assumptions** for series | **Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)**, **Windowed transforms** |
| **Computational cost** for raw DFT (`O(N²)`) | **Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)** (`O(N log N)`), cooley-tukey algorithm |
| **Nonlinear/adaptive signals** | **Hilbert-Huang Transform**, **Empirical Mode Decomposition** |

**Sampling Reality:** Digital FT requires the **Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem** (`f_s > 2f_max`) to avoid aliasing. Real-world systems use anti-aliasing filters before discretization.

---

### 🔗 Connection to Maxwell's Equations
Fourier analysis is the **natural language of linear wave physics**:
- Maxwell's equations are linear PDEs → FT converts spatial/temporal derivatives into algebraic factors (`∂/∂t → iω`, `∇ → i**k**`).
- The wave equation becomes `(ω²/c² - k²) F(**k**,ω) = 0` → instantly reveals dispersion relation `ω = c|**k**|`.
- Boundary value problems decompose into **eigenmodes** (Fourier modes), enabling analytical & numerical solutions.
- In modern electromagnetics, **FDTD** and **FEM** solvers rely on FFT-based spectral methods for efficiency.

---

### 💡 Why It Matters
- **Unifies perspectives:** Time/space ↔ frequency/wavenumber duality mirrors position/momentum in QM.
- **Diagonalizes linear operators:** Differentiation, convolution, and linear filters become multiplication.
- **Computational backbone:** FFT is among the top 10 algorithms of the 20th century; embedded in every digital device.
- **Deep mathematical structure:** Links to functional analysis (`L²` spaces), harmonic analysis, group theory (abelian groups), and signal processing theory.

The **Doppler effect** is the change in observed frequency (or wavelength) of a wave caused by relative motion between the source and the observer. It applies to all wave types but has distinct classical and relativistic forms.

---

### 🔍 Core Intuition
- **Approaching** → wavefronts compress → higher frequency / shorter wavelength
- **Receding** → wavefronts stretch → lower frequency / longer wavelength
- Only the **radial component** of relative velocity matters
- Magnitude of shift scales as `v/c`

---

### 📐 Classical Doppler (Mechanical Waves)
Requires a physical medium. Speed of wave `v` is fixed relative to the medium.

| Scenario | Observed Frequency |
|----------|-------------------|
| Observer moves toward stationary source | `f' = f \frac{v + v_o}{v}` |
| Observer moves away from stationary source | `f' = f \frac{v - v_o}{v}` |
| Source moves toward stationary observer | `f' = f \frac{v}{v - v_s}` |
| Source moves away from stationary observer | `f' = f \frac{v}{v + v_s}` |
| **General** | `f' = f \frac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s}` *(+ toward, − away)* |

**Wavelength shift:** `λ' = λ \frac{v \mp v_s}{v}`

---

### 🔬 Relativistic Doppler (Electromagnetic Waves)
No medium. Speed of light `c` is invariant. Time dilation and light-travel delay combine.

| Geometry | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| **Longitudinal** (radial motion) | `f' = f \sqrt{\frac{1 - \beta}{1 + \beta}}` (receding)<br>`f' = f \sqrt{\frac{1 + \beta}{1 - \beta}}` (approaching)<br>where `β = v/c` |
| **Transverse** (90° in observer frame) | `f' = f / \gamma`, `\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1-\beta^2}` *(pure time dilation)* |
| **Wavelength** | `λ' = λ \sqrt{\frac{1 \mp \beta}{1 \pm \beta}}` |
| **Redshift parameter** | `1 + z = \frac{λ_\text{obs}}{λ_\text{emit}} = \sqrt{\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}}` |

---

### 🔑 Key Properties
- **Only radial velocity matters**: Transverse motion produces relativistic time-dilation shift
- **Symmetric for light**: No preferred frame; depends only on relative speed
- **Linear approximation** (`v ≪ c`): `Δf/f ≈ ±v/c`
- **Frequency translation**: In signal processing, Doppler = time-stretching → spectrum shifts
