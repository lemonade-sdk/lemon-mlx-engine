**Fourier analysis** is a mathematical framework that decomposes complex functions or signals into simpler sinusoidal components (sines and cosines). It reveals the **frequency content** of a signal, shifting the perspective from the time or spatial domain to the **frequency domain**. This shift is one of the most powerful ideas in physics, engineering, and data science.

---

### 🔑 Core Idea
> **Any well-behaved function can be expressed as a sum (or integral) of pure frequencies.**

Just as you can mix red, green, and blue light to create any color, you can mix sine waves of different frequencies, amplitudes, and phases to reconstruct almost any periodic or transient signal.

---

### 📐 Mathematical Framework

#### 1. **Fourier Series** (for periodic functions)
If \( f(t) \) repeats every period \( T \), it can be written as:
\[
f(t) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left[ a_n \cos(n\omega_0 t) + b_n \sin(n\omega_0 t) \right]
\]
where \( \omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T} \) is the fundamental frequency.

**Coefficients** are extracted using orthogonality of sinusoids:
\[
a_n = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) \cos(n\omega_0 t) \, dt, \quad
b_n = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) \sin(n\omega_0 t) \, dt
\]

#### 2. **Fourier Transform (FT)** (for non-periodic, finite-energy signals)
Extends the series to continuous frequencies:
\[
\boxed{F(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t) e^{-i\omega t} \, dt}
\]
**Inverse FT:**
\[
f(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F(\omega) e^{i\omega t} \, d\omega
\]
*(Note: Conventions vary. Some place \(1/\sqrt{2\pi}\) in both, or use frequency \(f\) instead of angular frequency \(\omega\). The physics remains identical.)*

**Why Euler's formula?** \( e^{i\omega t} = \cos(\omega t) + i\sin(\omega t) \) lets us pack cosine/sine pairs into a single complex exponential, simplifying calculus and revealing phase information.

---

### 🔍 Key Concepts & Intuition

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Frequency Spectrum** | \( |F(\omega)| \) shows which frequencies are present; \( \arg(F(\omega)) \) gives their phase. |
| **Orthogonality** | Sines/cosines at different frequencies are "perpendicular" in function space, allowing clean separation of components. |
| **Linear Superposition** | FT is a linear operation: FT\( [af+bg] = a\,\text{FT}[f] + b\,\text{FT}[g] \). |
| **Time–Frequency Tradeoff** | A precise frequency measurement requires a long time window (Heisenberg–Gabor limit). |

---

### 🌍 Major Applications

| Field | Use Case |
|-------|----------|
| **Signal Processing** | Audio filtering, radio modulation, noise reduction, feature extraction |
| **Physics & Engineering** | Solving PDEs (heat, wave, Schrödinger), circuit analysis, optics (diffraction) |
| **Data Science** | Spectral clustering, time-series forecasting, CNNs (frequency-domain convolutions) |
| **Image Processing** | JPEG compression (block-based FT), edge detection, deblurring |
| **Communications** | OFDM (Wi-Fi, 4G/5G), FFT-based modulation/demodulation |

---

### 💻 From Theory to Practice: DFT & FFT

Continuous FTs are theoretical. Real-world data is discrete → we use the **Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT)**:
\[
X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n e^{-i 2\pi k n / N}, \quad k=0,\dots,N-1
\]
- **FFT (Fast Fourier Transform)** is an algorithm that computes the DFT in \( O(N \log N) \) time instead of \( O(N^2) \).
- Revolutionized engineering in the 1960s; now runs in every smartphone, medical scanner, and audio interface.

**Practical notes:**
- Requires sampling at \( f_s > 2f_{\max} \) (Nyquist–Shannon theorem)
- Windowing prevents spectral leakage
- Aliasing occurs if undersampled

---

### ⚠️ Limitations & Modern Extensions

| Limitation | Why it matters | Modern fix |
|------------|----------------|------------|
| Assumes global periodicity or infinite support | Poor for transient/non-stationary signals | **Short-Time FT** (STFT), **Wavelets** |
| Fixed frequency resolution | Can't resolve fast-changing frequencies well | **Multiresolution analysis** (wavelet packings) |
| Gibbs phenomenon | Oscillations near discontinuities | Smoothed kernels, rise–fall filters |

---

### 🔑 Core Intuition
- **Approaching** → waves compress → higher frequency (shorter wavelength)
- **Receding** → waves stretch → lower frequency (longer wavelength)
- Classic example: a siren’s pitch drops as it passes you.

---

### 📐 Mathematical Formulations

#### 1. **Classical (Acoustic) Doppler**
Requires a medium (e.g., air). Source and observer motion are *not* symmetric because the medium defines a reference frame.

\[
f_{\text{obs}} = f_{\text{src}} \left( \frac{v \pm v_{\text{obs}}}{v \mp v_{\text{src}}} \right)
\]
- \(v\) = wave speed in medium
- \(v_{\text{obs}}\) > 0 if observer moves *toward* source
- \(v_{\text{src}}\) > 0 if source moves *toward* observer
- Signs chosen to increase \(f_{\text{obs}}\) when approaching.

#### 2. **Relativistic (Electromagnetic) Doppler**
Light needs no medium. Only *relative* velocity matters, and time dilation is included.

**Longitudinal (motion along line of sight):**
\[
f_{\text{obs}} = f_{\text{src}} \sqrt{\frac{1 + \beta}{1 - \beta}} \quad (\text{approaching})
\]
\[
f_{\text{obs}} = f_{\text{src}} \sqrt{\frac{1 - \beta}{1 + \beta}} \quad (\text{receding})
\]
where \(\beta = v/c\), \(v\) = relative speed, \(c\) = speed of light.

**Transverse (motion perpendicular to line of sight):**
\[
f_{\text{obs}} = f_{\text{src}} \sqrt{1 - \beta^2} = \frac{f_{\text{src}}}{\gamma}
\]
*Purely relativistic* (time dilation), no classical analog.

---

### 🔍 Key Physics Distinctions

| Aspect | Acoustic (Sound) | Electromagnetic (Light) |
|--------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Medium needed? | Yes (defines reference) | No (Lorentz invariant) |
| Symmetric? | No (source vs observer ≠) | Yes (only relative \(v\) matters) |
| Max speed limit? | \(v_{\text{src}} < v_{\text{sound}}\) (shock waves ≠ Doppler) | \(v < c\) (relativistic formulas hold) |
| Time dilation? | Negligible | Included (transverse Doppler) |

---

### 📡 Signal Processing & Fourier Connection
In engineering, Doppler is a **frequency translation**:
- Time-scaling: \( t \to t(1 - \beta) \) (approx for \(v \ll c\))
- Frequency-scaling: \( f \to f/(1-\beta) \approx f(1+\beta) \)
- In the frequency domain (via Fourier transform), this is just a **shift**: \( F_{\text{obs}}(f) = F_{\text{src}}(f/(1+\beta)) \)

**Pulse-Doppler Radar** exploits this:
1. Transmit pulse → receive echo
2. Compute FT across pulse returns (slow-time)
3. Each velocity class appears at a distinct frequency bin
4. Filter by Doppler shift → isolate moving targets from clutter

### ⚠️ Common Misconceptions
- ❌ "Doppler happens because waves 'catch up' to the observer."  
  ✅ It's about *emission interval* vs *reception interval* in the observer's frame.
- ❌ "Light Doppler is just classical with \(c\) instead of \(v_{\text{sound}}\)."  
  ✅ Relativistic time dilation changes the formula and enables transverse Doppler.
- ❌ "Doppler shift changes wave amplitude."  
  ✅ It changes frequency/wavelength; intensity changes separately (e.g., relativistic beaming).

Here’s a complete, self-contained Python script that brings **Maxwell’s equations**, **Fourier analysis**, and the **Doppler effect** together in a unified simulation. It models a classic **Doppler Radar** system, which is the engineering realization of all three concepts.

### 📜 What the Code Does
1. **Maxwell** → Simulates EM wave propagation in vacuum using the 1D wave equation (direct reduction of Maxwell’s equations in source-free regions).
2. **Doppler** → Applies a frequency shift to simulate a moving target.
3. **Fourier** → Uses FFT to extract the beat frequency and recover the target’s velocity.

### 💻 Python Implementation
```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# ============================
# 1. PHYSICS PARAMETERS
# ============================
c = 3e8                # Speed of light (m/s) -> c = 1/sqrt(mu0*eps0) from Maxwell
f0 = 10e9              # Radar carrier frequency (10 GHz)
v_true = 50.0          # Target radial velocity (m/s)
R0 = 100.0             # Initial target range (m)
fs = 2e6               # Sampling frequency (2 MHz)
T = 1.5                # Total simulation time (s)
t = np.arange(0, T, 1/fs)

# ============================
# 2. MAXWELL -> WAVE EQUATION SOLUTION
# ============================
# In source-free vacuum, Maxwell's equations reduce to the 1D wave equation:
# ∂²E/∂t² = c² ∂²E/∂x²
# Solution: E(x,t) = f(x-ct) + g(x+ct)
# Radar exploits the round-trip solution: transmitted -> reflects -> received

# Transmitted carrier wave
s_tx = np.cos(2 * np.pi * f0 * t)

# Round-trip propagation delay
tau = 2 * R0 / c

# ============================
# 3. DOPPLER -> FREQUENCY SHIFT
# ============================
# Classical Doppler for radar (v << c): fd = 2*v*f0/c
fd_theoretical = 2 * v_true * f0 / c
s_rx = np.cos(2 * np.pi * (f0 + fd_theoretical) * (t - tau))

# ============================
# 4. FOURIER -> SPECTRAL ANALYSIS
# ============================
# Radar mixes (multiplies) Tx and Rx to downconvert to baseband
s_beat = s_tx * s_rx

# Apply a simple low-pass filter to remove the ~2f0 term (standard in radar)
kernel = np.ones(50)/50
s_beat_lpf = np.convolve(s_beat, kernel, mode='same')

# FFT spectrum
N = len(t)
freqs = np.fft.fftfreq(N, d=1/fs)
spectrum = np.abs(np.fft.fft(s_beat_lpf))
spectrum = spectrum[:N//2]  # One-sided spectrum
freqs = freqs[:N//2]

# Extract dominant frequency -> estimate velocity
idx = np.argmax(spectrum)
fd_est = freqs[idx]
v_est = fd_est * c / (2 * f0)

print(f"{'True Velocity:':<15} {v_true:>8.2f} m/s")
print(f"{'Est. Velocity:':<15} {v_est:>8.2f} m/s")
print(f"{'Doppler Shift:':<15} {fd_theoretical:>8.1f} Hz")

# ============================
# 5. VISUALIZATION
# ============================
plt.figure(figsize=(12, 8))

# Time domain
plt.subplot(2, 1, 1)
plt.plot(t[:500], s_tx[:500], label='Transmitted', alpha=0.7)
plt.plot(t[:500], s_rx[:500], label='Received (Doppler shifted)', alpha=0.7)
plt.title('Time Domain: EM Wave Propagation (Maxwell Wave Equation Solution)')
plt.xlabel('Time (s)'); plt.ylabel('Amplitude')
plt.legend(); plt.grid(True)

# Frequency domain
plt.subplot(2, 1, 2)
plt.plot(freqs, spectrum, 'b-', linewidth=1.5)
plt.axvline(fd_theoretical, color='r', linestyle='--', label=f'Expected Doppler: {fd_theoretical:.1f} Hz')
plt.title('Frequency Domain: Fourier Spectrum (Doppler Extraction)')
plt.xlabel('Frequency (Hz)'); plt.ylabel('Amplitude')
plt.legend(); plt.grid(True)
plt.xlim(0, 5000)  # Zoom in on low-frequency beat signal

plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```
