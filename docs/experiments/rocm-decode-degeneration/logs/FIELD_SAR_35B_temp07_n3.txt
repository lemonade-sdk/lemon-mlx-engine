Maxwell's equations are a set of four partial differential equations that form the foundation of **classical electromagnetism**. They describe how electric and magnetic fields are generated, how they interact, and how they propagate through space. Together, they unify electricity, magnetism, and optics into a single coherent theory.

Here’s a structured overview:

---

### 🔹 The Four Equations (Differential Form in Vacuum)
Physicists typically write them using vector calculus notation. Each equation has a physical meaning that can be understood without advanced math:

1. **Gauss’s Law for Electricity**  
   `∇ · E = ρ / ε₀`  
   *Meaning:* Electric charges (`ρ`) are the source of electric fields (`E`). Field lines diverge from positive charges and converge on negative ones. No "magnetic charge" exists (see #2).

2. **Gauss’s Law for Magnetism**  
   `∇ · B = 0`  
   *Meaning:* Magnetic field lines (`B`) always form closed loops. There are no isolated magnetic poles (monopoles) in classical electromagnetism.

3. **Faraday’s Law of Induction**  
   `∇ × E = −∂B/∂t`  
   *Meaning:* A changing magnetic field induces a circulating electric field. This is the principle behind electric generators and transformers.

4. **Ampère–Maxwell Law**  
   `∇ × B = μ₀J + μ₀ε₀ ∂E/∂t`  
   *Meaning:* Magnetic fields are produced by electric currents (`J`) **and** by changing electric fields. The second term (added by Maxwell) is called the *displacement current*. It’s crucial for predicting electromagnetic waves.

**Symbols:**  
`E` = electric field, `B` = magnetic field, `ρ` = charge density, `J` = current density, `ε₀` = vacuum permittivity, `μ₀` = vacuum permeability.

---

### 🔸 What They Reveal: Electromagnetic Waves
When you combine these equations (usually by taking curls and using vector identities), you get a **wave equation** for both `E` and `B`:
```
∇²E = μ₀ε₀ ∂²E/∂t²    and    ∇²B = μ₀ε₀ ∂²B/∂t²
```
The speed of these waves is:
```
c = 1 / √(μ₀ε₀) ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s
```
This matched the known speed of light, leading Maxwell to conclude that **light itself is an electromagnetic wave**. This unified optics with electromagnetism and predicted the entire electromagnetic spectrum (radio, microwaves, X-rays, etc.).

---

- Predecessors: Coulomb, Gauss, Faraday, and Ampère discovered individual principles in the 18th–19th centuries.
- James Clerk Maxwell synthesized these in the 1860s, added the displacement current term, and showed that light is an EM wave.
- Oliver Heaviside and Heinrich Hertz later reformulated Maxwell’s original 20 equations (in 20 variables) into the compact 4-equation vector form used today.
- The equations are fully consistent with Einstein’s special relativity; in fact, they motivated its development.

---

### ⚡ Why They Matter
- **Engineering & Technology:** Every electrical system, wireless communication, radar, MRI, lasers, and power grid relies on them.
- **Physics Foundation:** They are a cornerstone of classical field theory and directly inspired gauge theories and quantum electrodynamics (QED).
- **Predictive Power:** Accurately describe macroscopic electromagnetic phenomena to extraordinary precision.

### ⚠️ Limitations & Modern View

Fourier analysis is one of the most transformative ideas in mathematics and physics. At its core, it answers a deceptively simple question:

> **Can any complicated signal or function be broken down into simple, pure tones?**

The answer is yes, and doing so unlocks powerful ways to analyze, compress, filter, and solve problems across nearly every scientific and engineering discipline.

---

### 🔑 The Core Idea
Fourier analysis asserts that **complex patterns can be constructed from (or decomposed into) sums of sine and cosine waves** (or, equivalently, complex exponentials). Each component has a specific frequency, amplitude, and phase. By shifting your perspective from the *time/space domain* to the *frequency domain*, many messy problems become elegant algebraic ones.

---

### 📐 Two Main Branches

#### 1. **Fourier Series** (Periodic Functions)
If a function `f(t)` repeats every `T` seconds, it can be written as an infinite sum of harmonically related sinusoids:
```
f(t) = a₀ + Σₙ₌₁^∞ [aₙ cos(2πnt/T) + bₙ sin(2πnt/T)]
```
Or more compactly using complex exponentials:
```
f(t) = Σₙ₌₋∞^∞ cₙ e^(i2πnt/T)
```
The coefficients `cₙ` are found by projecting `f(t)` onto each basis frequency:
```
cₙ = (1/T) ∫₀^T f(t) e^(-i2πnt/T) dt
```
*Use case:* Musical tones, AC power systems, vibrating strings, any repeating waveform.

#### 2. **Fourier Transform (FT)** (Aperiodic/Continuous Signals)
For non-repeating signals, the sum becomes an integral. The FT maps a function from the time domain to the frequency domain:
```
F(ω) = ∫₋∞^∞ f(t) e^(-iωt) dt          (Forward FT)
f(t) = (1/2π) ∫₋∞^∞ F(ω) e^(iωt) dω   (Inverse FT)
```
- `ω = 2πf` is angular frequency (radians/sec)
- `F(ω)` tells you *which frequencies* are present and *how strong* they are (magnitude) and *when they peak* (phase)

*Use case:* Radar pulses, genetic sequences, heat diffusion, quantum wavefunctions, any non-periodic signal.

---

### ⚙️ Key Properties & Theorems
| Property | Meaning | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------|----------------|
| **Linearity** | FT(`af + bg`) = `aF + bG` | Enables superposition; systems respond to components independently |
| **Convolution Theorem** | FT(`f * g`) = `F(ω)·G(ω)` | Turns differential equations into algebra; basis of digital filtering |
| **Parseval’s Identity** | `∫|f(t)|² dt = (1/2π)∫|F(ω)|² dω` | Energy/power is conserved between domains; vital for signal processing |
| **Duality & Symmetry** | Real `f(t)` → `F(-ω) = F*(ω)` | Halves computation; reveals physical constraints |
| **Uncertainty Principle** | `Δt · Δω ≥ 1/2` | You can't simultaneously know exact time and exact frequency; fundamental to signal resolution |

---

### 💻 Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) & FFT
Real-world data is sampled, not continuous. The **DFT** computes frequency content from `N` discrete points:
```
Xₖ = Σₙ₌₀^N-1 xₙ e^(-i2πkn/N),   k = 0,1,...,N-1
```
- **FFT (Fast Fourier Transform)**: An algorithm that computes the DFT in `O(N log N)` instead of `O(N²)`. Invented in 1965, it revolutionized digital signal processing, enabling real-time audio, imaging, and communications.

The **Doppler effect** is the change in observed frequency (or wavelength) of a wave when the source and observer are in relative motion. It applies to all wave phenomena: sound, light, water, and even matter waves.

---

### 🌊 Intuition
Imagine a boat moving across a lake, dropping pebbles at regular intervals:
- **In front of the boat**, wave crests bunch together → shorter wavelength, higher frequency.
- **Behind the boat**, crests spread out → longer wavelength, lower frequency.

For sound, this is why an ambulance siren sounds higher-pitched as it approaches and drops in pitch as it passes. For light, it manifests as a **blueshift** (approaching) or **redshift** (receding).

---

### 📊 The Math

#### 🔹 Classical (Sound/Mechanical Waves)
Requires a physical medium. The formula is **asymmetric** because motion is measured relative to the medium:
```
f' = f · (v ± v_o) / (v ∓ v_s)
```
- `f` = emitted frequency, `f'` = observed frequency
- `v` = speed of wave in medium (e.g., ~343 m/s for sound in air)
- `v_o` = observer speed (toward source: `+`, away: `−`)
- `v_s` = source speed (toward observer: `−`, away: `+`)

*Sign convention is critical*: approach increases `f'`, recession decreases it.

#### 🔹 Relativistic (Light/EM Waves)
No medium is needed. Special relativity replaces the classical formula:
```
f' = f · √[(1 + β) / (1 − β)]   (approach)
f' = f · √[(1 − β) / (1 + β)]   (recession)
```
where `β = v/c` (v = relative speed, c = speed of light).

For `v << c`, this simplifies to the familiar approximation used in astronomy:
```
Δλ/λ ≈ v/c   or   Δf/f ≈ −v/c
```
- `Δλ` = wavelength shift, `λ` = rest wavelength
- Positive `Δλ` → redshift (receding), negative → blueshift (approaching)

---

### 🌍 Real-World Applications

| Field | Use Case |
|-------|----------|
| **Astronomy** | Measuring galaxy recession (Hubble expansion), exoplanet detection (radial velocity method), stellar rotation |
| **Meteorology** | Doppler weather radar tracks precipitation velocity → predicts storm rotation, tornadoes, wind fields |
| **Medicine** | Doppler ultrasound measures blood flow velocity, heart valve function, fetal monitoring |
| **Telecom/Radar** | Police speed guns, air traffic control, satellite tracking, automotive adaptive cruise control |
| **Audio/Synthesis** | Virtualization, synth effects, audio engineering (pitch correction, phase alignment) |

---

### 🔗 Links to Your Previous Topics

#### 📡 **Maxwell & EM Waves**
- The Doppler shift for light is **not** the same as for sound because EM waves obey relativity, not Galilean mechanics.
- Maxwell’s equations predict EM waves travel at `c` in all inertial frames, which is why the relativistic formula replaces the classical one.
- The shift is a direct consequence of wavefront spacing changing in a Lorentz-transformed frame.

#### 📊 **Fourier Analysis**
- In practice, we **measure** Doppler shift by comparing the Fourier spectrum of the transmitted signal with the received signal.
- The shift appears as a **frequency offset** in the FT domain: `f' = f + Δf`
- For pulsed/radar systems, the **Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)** on successive pulses reveals velocity distributions (Doppler spectrum).
- For non-stationary signals, **Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)** or **wavelets** track how velocity changes over time.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# 🌐 PHYSICAL PARAMETERS
# ==========================================
c = 3e8                # Speed of light (m/s)
f_tx = 10e9            # Transmit frequency: 10 GHz (microwave band)
v = 120                # Target velocity: 120 m/s (~432 km/h)
t = np.linspace(0, 1e-3, 10000)  # 1 ms observation window
fs = 1/np.mean(np.diff(t))     # Sampling rate (~10 MHz)

# ==========================================
# 📡 STEP 1: MAXWELL'S EQUATIONS → WAVE GENERATION
# ==========================================
# Maxwell predicts EM waves propagate at c, oscillate at f_tx,

s_tx = np.sin(2 * np.pi * f_tx * t)

# ==========================================
# 🚀 STEP 2: DOPPLER EFFECT → MOTION-INDUCED SHIFT
# ==========================================
# For v << c, round-trip Doppler shift:
f_d = 2 * v / c * f_tx  # Beat frequency in Hz
f_rx = f_tx + f_d       # Received frequency (approaching target)

# Received signal (simplified: same amplitude, shifted frequency)
s_rx = np.sin(2 * np.pi * f_rx * t)

# ==========================================

freqs_pos = freqs[pos_mask]
S_mag = np.abs(S_beat)[pos_mask]

f_d_est = freqs_pos[np.argmax(S_mag)]

print(f"✅ True Doppler shift: {f_d:.2f} Hz")
print(f"✅ FFT peak shift:     {f_d_est:.2f} Hz")
print(f"✅ Estimated velocity: {f_d_est * c / 2:.1f} m/s")

# ==========================================

axes[0].grid(True, alpha=0.3)
